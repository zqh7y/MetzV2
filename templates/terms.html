Metz is a small app for finding and organising meetups in person and online. By creating an account you agree to what is below. It is written plainly on purpose — if something here is unclear, ask before you sign up.

Who can use it

You need to be at least 13, and old enough where you live to agree to terms like these on your own. One account per person. Keep your password to yourself — anything done from your account is treated as done by you.

Meeting people is your own risk

This is the part that matters most. Metz lists meetups; it does not vet the people at them. Nobody here is background-checked, and a show-up rate or a trusted badge is a record of past behaviour, not a safety guarantee.

Meet in public places, tell someone where you are going, and leave if you feel uncomfortable. You are responsible for your own safety and for how you behave towards others. Metz is not a party to anything that happens at a meetup and cannot be held responsible for it.

What you post

You keep ownership of the meetings, descriptions and profile details you write. By posting them you allow Metz to display them to other users, which is the whole point of the app.

Do not post anything that:

is illegal, or organises something illegal
harasses, threatens or targets somebody
is hateful towards a group of people
is sexual content, or is aimed at children
is spam, advertising, or a scam
impersonates somebody else
shares someone's private details without their agreement

Reporting and moderation

Every meeting and profile can be reported from inside the app, and you can block anyone you would rather not see. Reports are reviewed and content that breaks the rules above is removed. New meetings are checked before they appear publicly.

Accounts that repeatedly break these rules can be suspended or removed without warning. There is no appeal process — this is a small project, not a company with a support desk.

Turning up

Joining a meeting is a commitment other people plan around. Your show-up rate is built from meetings you said you would attend and then did or did not. It is visible to others, and that is deliberate — it is what makes a join mean something.

Leaving

You can delete your account at any time from Settings. That removes your profile, the meetings you created, and your place in meetings you joined. It cannot be undone. What happens to your data is described in the privacy policy.

No promises about uptime

Metz is provided as it is. It is a personal project and it may be slow, go down, lose data, or stop existing. Do not rely on it for anything important. To the extent the law allows, there is no liability for loss arising from using it.
